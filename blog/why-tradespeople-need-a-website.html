Why Every Tradesperson Needs a Website in 2026 (Not Just Social Media)

A lot of electricians, plumbers, builders, and other tradespeople get by on an Instagram page, a Facebook business profile, and word of mouth. It works, up to a point. But relying on social media alone leaves real gaps that a proper website closes, and those gaps usually show up as lost jobs you never even hear about.

Social media reach is rented, not owned

Every platform you post on can change its algorithm, reduce your organic reach, restrict your account, or disappear entirely. Your website is the one piece of your online presence you actually control. Nobody can deprioritise it in a feed or change how it's shown to people.

People still Google before they call

Even someone who found you on Instagram will often search your business name, or "electrician near me," before getting in touch. If there's nothing to find, or what they find looks unfinished, that hesitation can be enough to lose the job to a competitor who does show up properly.

A website builds trust fast

A clear, professional website signals that a business is established, takes itself seriously, and is worth trusting with a job in someone's home. A social profile alone doesn't carry the same weight. Anyone can post photos, but a proper website takes intent.

It works while you're on a job

Unlike a social post that gets buried within hours, a website keeps working around the clock. Someone can find your services, see your work, and get your number at 9pm on a Sunday, while you're nowhere near your phone.

SEO compounds, social posts don't

A well-built website with proper on-page SEO keeps showing up in search results months and years after it's built. A social post has a shelf life of a day or two before it's buried under everything posted since. Over time, a website is the asset that keeps paying you back.

What a minimum viable trade website looks like

  • A clear list of the services you offer
  • The areas you cover
  • Photos of real work you've done
  • A handful of genuine reviews or testimonials
  • An obvious way to call, message, or request a quote

That's genuinely it. You don't need twenty pages or a complicated build. You need one that does those five things properly.
